# Script playbook

A Reel script is not an essay read aloud. It is a sequence of short beats, each one giving the viewer a reason to stay for the next.

## Anatomy (for 20 to 35 seconds)

| Part | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0 to 2 s | Stop the scroll. Make a promise or open a gap. |
| Context or stakes | 2 to 6 s | Why this matters to the viewer, in one beat. |
| Payload | 6 to 25 s | The answer, in two to four short beats. One idea. |
| Turn | near the end | A surprise, a callback to the hook, or the real answer. |
| Close and CTA | last 3 s | One clear action: comment a word, save, share, follow. |

Every beat should be one short spoken line, ideally under 12 words in Uzbek. If a line needs two breaths, split it.

## Hooks

Write three, each a different type, then recommend one. Test each by asking: would a stranger stop scrolling at this line with the sound off, from the on screen text alone?

Hook types and Uzbek patterns (formal "siz"):

- **Question that stings.** "Sun'iy intellekt ishingizni olib qo'yadimi?" Works when the question is already in the viewer's head.
- **Mistake.** "Ish topishda 90 foiz odam shu xatoni qiladi." Only use a number you can support, or drop the number.
- **Counterintuitive claim.** "Diplomingiz sizni ishga olmaydi." Then earn it in the next beat.
- **Challenge or timer.** "Parolingizni 10 soniyada buzishadi. Tekshirib ko'ring." Creates an immediate stake.
- **Scene.** A relatable moment in dialogue form: "Ona: qachon uylanasan?" Works for humor sketches.
- **Result first.** Show the end state in the first second, then say how.

Avoid starting with greetings, introductions, or "bugun biz gaplashamiz". Start inside the story.

## Retention rules

- Change something every 2 to 3 seconds: new image, new camera move, new text, new sound. Not all at once, one at a time.
- Open a gap early and close it late. A question asked in the hook should be answered only near the end.
- Use specific words. "Telefoningizga kuniga 4 soat 12 daqiqa qarayapsiz" beats "juda ko'p vaqt".
- Put the strongest line in the second half, not the middle. Give the viewer a reason to reach it.
- End so the loop feels natural: the last line can lead back to the first frame.
- Cut hedging. If a sentence can be removed without losing the idea, remove it.

## Structures that work

**List of N (3 to 5 items).** Hook with the count and the stakes, one item per beat, the surprising item last, a CTA that asks for the viewer's own item. Show the enumeration as a plain page with items writing in, without the character, so nothing competes with the text.

**Myth then truth.** Claim, "mana bu noto'g'ri", the reason, what to do instead.

**Three step answer.** Question hook, "bu uchta qadamda", steps one to three, outcome.

**Story with a twist.** Setup in two beats, escalation in one, twist, one line of meaning.

**Dialogue sketch.** Two or three characters, a recognizable situation, one escalation, one punchline. Keep each line to one breath. Give each character a distinct way of speaking so the audio alone tells them apart.

**Before and after.** Show the bad state, the change, the good state, and the one rule behind it.

## Voice direction

Write the script the way it will be said, not the way it reads.

- Mark stress with capitals or a note, for example "bil**ma**sangiz" stretched for effect.
- Mark pauses with a slash and big pauses with two slashes.
- Give each line a tone note: curious, urgent, warm, deadpan.
- For ElevenLabs, add audio tags at the line start, such as `[excited]`, `[whispers]`, `[reassuring]`, `[sighs]`, and keep them sparing.
- Default delivery speed in the final video is 1.25x, so write lines that still sound natural when slightly faster, with no long tongue twisters.

## Caption and hashtags

- First line of the caption repeats the hook in different words, because the first line shows before "more".
- Two or three short lines, then a call to action.
- Five to eight hashtags: two broad, three niche, one or two local. Check that none is banned or irrelevant.
- Comment prompt: ask for one word or one number, which is easier to answer than an opinion.

## Fact and rights check

- Every date, figure, name and claim in the script gets a source. Put the links in your notes, not in the video.
- Do not use real people's likeness or voices, copyrighted characters, or brand logos as characters.
- Medical, legal, financial, and security claims must be accurate and framed responsibly. When in doubt, soften or cut.
